In this episode, Dave and Jamison answer these questions:

1. I'm 8 minutes into a 45 minute interview with a candidate that is not a good fit. What should I do? I eagerly await your response.

2. Hello! I sure am neat, and so are you.
   
   I have been working as a software engineer at a Very Big American Tech Company for over 6 years. I joined as a junior, promoted within a year, and have recently (in the last year) changed from a mainly software organisation to being in a software team for a non-software organisation (think hardware delivery, cloud scaling, that kinda stuff). All of this has been from a non-American location.
   
   Here is my dilemma - the scope for a promotion in this new organisation is murky and undefined, as the vast majority of promotions have been for our non-software engineers. While there are company-wide guidelines for promotion, I have heard that there has been push back on other promotions to get to my current level, because "we don't know what software promotions look like yet".
   
   My manager also did not 'grow up' in this organisation, having joined only in the last few months.
   
   From what I can tell of the recent promotions, it's all been about delivering new software services that address our hardware delivery needs. But our software is so garbage! Littered with abandoned micro services, more automated scripts than you can think of, and enough tech debt to make Claude cry. I don't know if I want to add to this mess with promotion-driven development, even though I feel that's the only way forward.
   
   In our current climate, finding another job is not really feasible. I enjoy my job otherwise, and am reluctant to go.
   
   Should I just shut up and do the work I'm asked, and enjoy what I can? Should I push to deliver $NEW\_CRAP\_SERVICE and be cursed at by future developers? Does it really even matter?
   
   Thanks. I appreciate your advice, and hope I have the wisdom to do what's best for me.
