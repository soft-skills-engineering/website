In this episode, Dave and Jamison answer these questions:

1. Hi Dave & Jamison! I've been listening for almost a year now and have worked through nearly your entire back catalog in reverse. The podcast has genuinely helped me become a better engineer, so thank you!
   
   I'm two years into my software engineer career at a large infrastructure company. I was promoted to SWE II last year, and this year my manager told me she felt my work was promotion-worthy, but because I'd just been promoted and she had others on the team to consider, she wants to target me for next year's cycle instead. She gave me a spot bonus and said she'd try to make up for it in my raise.
   
   I understand and appreciate the honesty, but I'm also a little disappointed because I've worked hard to hit every milestone on the roadmap she gave me, and career progression does matter to me.
   
   My question is: How should I navigate the next year? What should I focus my 1:1s on? Should I push for a mid-year promotion if that's an option, or instead focus on building an undeniable case for next year? How would you approach this if you were in my shoes?

2. Hey Dave and Jamison,
   
   Long time listener. First time question asker :).
   
   I recently joined a major tech company. Before this, I worked at a mid-sized company with about 200 engineers. In my old role, I built customer-facing products. The work was challenging and exciting, and I genuinely enjoyed it. I ended up moving to a new city to be closer to family and started looking for a job. The job search was brutal. Honestly, by the end of it, I would have accepted just about anything. Luckily, a major tech company extended an offer. The money was great, and I thought it would be a huge plus to have a major tech name on my resume. It is a fully remote role, but they did mention that they could go hybrid in the future.
   
   I knew ahead of time that the team built internal tools instead of customer-facing products. But after joining, I realized most of what we do is closer to operations than actual software engineering. I know I should have screened the team better. I was just so relieved to get a good offer from a top company that I accepted without thinking twice. I am not enjoying the work at all.
   
   I am not planning to quit immediately. However, I want to be ready to leave in about a year or earlier if they decide to lay us all off. I want to make sure I utilize my time here as best as possible. Do you have any suggestions on how I can best position myself for an internal transfer or an eventual external move?
   
   Also, I have been in this role for close to 2 months now. I did a 1-1 with the manager in my first week, but after that, have not had any 1-1s. He has not scheduled anything. I was thinking of scheduling it myself but not sure if that can be seen as too forward.
   
   Btw this is outside US, if that matters :)
   
   Thanks again for your advice and laughs.
