In this episode, Dave and Jamison answer these questions:

1. Hey Dave and Jamison.
   
   I don't know what to do with the amount of the AI slop I have to review and I'm getting close to burnout.
   
   I have a lot of context on my project, so my colleagues see me as a point of reference. Additionally, at a past job, I was a tech lead and could easily cover for TL if I wanted to (which I don't) and I feel like the TL of my project and some of my teammates are taking advantage of this.
   
   Every day I receive a lot of low quality documents to review. For example, our TL creates sloppy project planning documents and tickets and sends them to me for comments. Since they contain so many errors, sometimes including obvious security or data compatibility issues, or just text that is unrelated to the project, I end up basically rewriting everything.
   
   The same thing happens with code reviews. Some code is obviously AI-generated slop with lots of duplication and errors, that in many cases misses acceptance criteria or has doc strings filled with buzzwords and adjectives. I usually add a lot of comments per PR and I know the author sends them directly to their LLM and then pastes back a verbose answer. As a result, I end up in endless conversation loops.
   
   I've tried talking to my colleagues one-on-one, explaining how much work they are delegating to me. I've asked them to review their own work first and explained how exhausted I am, hoping they would show some compassion. But nothing has changed, because everyone seems to be in survival mode in this era of layoffs and I feel like they have become comfortable with me doing their work. I've also tried talking about this in retrospectives and tech team meetings, but the TL and tech directors did not support me because AI usage is encouraged. Instead, they said that they really appreciate my feedback because I have so much experience and thanked me for doing such excellent code reviews.
   
   I also end up making most of the project's tech decisions, I am the sole contributor to the project's documentation and my vacation is mapped out in the roadmap as low delivery period. 😢
   
   I like the project and I'm terrified to switch jobs in these times. But every day, it gets harder and harder to comment on obvious slop written without care without sounding rude or sarcastic. Sometimes I find myself still angry after work.
   
   Is it too late to set the boundaries?
